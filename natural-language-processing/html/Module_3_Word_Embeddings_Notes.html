Topic 25: The Distributional Hypothesis & Representation Paradigms

The foundational axiom of modern statistical semantics is Firth's (1957) Distributional Hypothesis: "You shall know a word by the company it keeps." Words occurring in identical linguistic contexts share similar semantic properties.

Representation Dimensionality & Geometry Key Computational Limitation
One-Hot Encoding Sparse $|V|$-dimensional binary vector with single 1 at word index (e.g., $[0, 0, 1, 0, \dots]^T$). Orthogonal vectors: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{cat}}^T \mathbf{x}_{\text{dog}} = 0$. Zero inherent semantic similarity; massive curse of dimensionality.
Count-Based VSM (TF-IDF / PPMI) Sparse, high-dimensional co-occurrence matrix transformed via statistical association metrics. Requires SVD dimensionality reduction; computationally expensive $O(|V|^2)$ scaling.
Distributed Dense Embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe) Dense, low-dimensional real-valued vectors $\mathbb{R}^d$ ($d \in [100, 300]$). Learned via self-supervised continuous neural optimization; captures geometric analogies ($\mathbf{v}_{\text{King}} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{Man}} + \mathbf{v}_{\text{Woman}} \approx \mathbf{v}_{\text{Queen}}$).

Topic 26: Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) & TF-IDF

Given word $w$ and context word $c$, Pointwise Mutual Information measures how much more often they co-occur than expected under random chance:

$$\mathbf{\text{PMI}(w, c) = \log_2 \frac{P(w, c)}{P(w)P(c)} = \log_2 \frac{C(w, c) \cdot N}{\sum_{c'} C(w, c') \sum_{w'} C(w', c)}}$$ $$\mathbf{\text{Positive PMI (PPMI): } \text{PPMI}(w, c) = \max(0, \text{PMI}(w, c))}$$

Topic 27: Word2Vec — Continuous Bag of Words (CBOW) & Skip-Gram

Mikolov et al. (2013) proposed two complementary shallow two-layer neural architectures for learning dense word embeddings from large unannotated corpora:

🧠 CBOW vs. Skip-Gram Architectures
  • CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words): Predicts the target center word $w_t$ given context words within window $c$: $[w_{t-c}, \dots, w_{t-1}, w_{t+1}, \dots, w_{t+c}]$. Context vectors are averaged: $\mathbf{h} = \frac{1}{2c}\sum \mathbf{v}_{w_{t+j}}$. Fast training; excellent representation for frequent words.
  • Skip-Gram: Predicts the surrounding context words given the center word $w_t$. Slower, but superior for small datasets and rare words.

Mathematical Formulation of Skip-Gram with Negative Sampling (SGNS)

Standard softmax requires computing $\sum_{w=1}^{|V|} \exp(\mathbf{v}'_w \cdot \mathbf{v}_c)$ across millions of vocabulary words—a prohibitive computational bottleneck. Negative Sampling re-frames multi-class classification as binary logistic regression:

$$\mathbf{\mathcal{L}_{\text{SGNS}}(\mathbf{\theta}) = \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{-c \le j \le c, j \neq 0} \left[ \log \sigma(\mathbf{v}'_{w_{t+j}} \cdot \mathbf{v}_{w_t}) + \sum_{k=1}^K \mathbb{E}_{w_{n_k} \sim P_n(w)} \left[ \log \sigma(-\mathbf{v}'_{w_{n_k}} \cdot \mathbf{v}_{w_t}) \right] \right]}$$ $$\text{where } \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}, \quad \text{Noise Distribution: } P_n(w) = \frac{U(w)^{3/4}}{\sum_{w'} U(w')^{3/4}}$$

The unigram exponent $3/4$ raises the relative sampling probability of rare words relative to ubiquitous stop words.

Topic 28: GloVe — Global Vectors for Word Representation

Pennington et al. (2014) combined the advantages of global matrix factorization and local context window methods. GloVe observes that semantic ratios of co-occurrence probabilities encode linguistic properties:

$$\mathbf{J = \sum_{i=1}^{|V|} \sum_{j=1}^{|V|} f(X_{ij}) \left( \mathbf{w}_i^T \mathbf{\tilde{w}}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j - \log X_{ij} \right)^2}$$ $$\text{Weighting Function: } f(x) = \begin{cases} (x / x_{\text{max}})^\alpha & \text{if } x < x_{\text{max}} \quad (\alpha = 0.75, x_{\text{max}} = 100) \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

Topic 29: FastText — Subword Character N-Grams

Bojanowski et al. (2017) extended Skip-Gram by representing each word $w$ as a set of character n-grams bounded by `<` and `>`:

$$\text{For word } \text{"where"} \ (n=3): \ \{\text{}, \text{}\}$$ $$\mathbf{\mathbf{v}_w = \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}_w} \mathbf{z}_g}$$

This enables FastText to compute robust vector representations for Out-Of-Vocabulary (OOV) words (e.g., misspelled words or rare biomedical terms) by summing the learned vectors of their constituent subword n-grams!

Topic 30: Master University Examination Question Bank (Module 3)

Q1. Explain Hierarchical Softmax in Word2Vec and how it reduces complexity from $O(|V|)$ to $O(\log_2 |V|)$. (8 Marks)
Hierarchical Softmax (Morin & Bengio 2005) represents the output vocabulary as the leaf nodes of a binary Huffman coding tree where frequent words have shorter branch depths. Instead of evaluating $|V|$ output logits, the model evaluates the probability of taking a left vs. right branch at each internal node $n$ along the root-to-leaf path $p(w) = (n_1, \dots, n_L)$:
$$P(w \mid w_I) = \prod_{j=1}^{L(w)-1} \sigma\left( [\![ n(w, j+1) = \text{ch}(n(w, j)) ]\!] \cdot \mathbf{\theta}_j^T \mathbf{v}_{w_I} \right)$$ Because the average depth in a Huffman tree is $O(\log_2 |V|)$, training complexity drops exponentially from $O(10^6)$ to $\approx 20$ operations per token!
Q2. Explain Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Evaluation of Word Embeddings. (8 Marks)
• Intrinsic Evaluation: Evaluates word vector quality directly on isolated semantic benchmark tasks (WordSim-353, SimLex-999 correlation with human semantic similarity ratings; syntactic/semantic word analogy accuracy: $a : b :: c : d \implies \arg\max_x \cos(\mathbf{v}_x, \mathbf{v}_b - \mathbf{v}_a + \mathbf{v}_c)$). Fast and model-agnostic, but high intrinsic scores do not guarantee downstream task gains.
• Extrinsic Evaluation: Plugs word embeddings as frozen/fine-tuned feature extractors into real-world downstream NLP pipelines (Named Entity Recognition, Sentiment Classification, Machine Translation) and measures end-task metrics (F1-score, BLEU, Accuracy). Computationally expensive and task-specific.
Q3. Detail the Cosine Similarity Metric and why Euclidean Distance is inappropriate for raw text term vectors. (6 Marks)
$$\text{Cosine Similarity: } \cos(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2}$$ Euclidean distance $\|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\|_2$ is severely confounded by document length—a 10-page document discussing Biology and a 1-paragraph summary will have massive Euclidean distance purely due to scalar term frequency magnitudes. Cosine similarity normalizes vector lengths to unit norm ($L_2$), measuring strictly the angle between semantic directions independent of document length!

Topic 31: Mathematical Proof & Gradient Derivation of Word2Vec SGNS

For a single center word $c$ and true context word $o$ with $K$ negative samples $\{n_1, \dots, n_K\}$ drawn from $P_n(w)$:

$$\mathbf{\mathcal{L} = \log \sigma(\mathbf{u}_o^T \mathbf{v}_c) + \sum_{k=1}^K \log \sigma(-\mathbf{u}_{n_k}^T \mathbf{v}_c)}$$
🏛️ Derivation of Parameter Gradients with Respect to Word Vectors

Recall that $\sigma'(z) = \sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))$ and $\frac{d}{dz}\log \sigma(z) = 1 - \sigma(z)$, while $\frac{d}{dz}\log \sigma(-z) = -\sigma(z)$.

1. Gradient with respect to true context vector $\mathbf{u}_o$:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{u}_o} = (1 - \sigma(\mathbf{u}_o^T \mathbf{v}_c)) \mathbf{v}_c}$$

2. Gradient with respect to negative context vector $\mathbf{u}_{n_k}$:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{u}_{n_k}} = -\sigma(\mathbf{u}_{n_k}^T \mathbf{v}_c) \mathbf{v}_c}$$

3. Gradient with respect to center word vector $\mathbf{v}_c$:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{v}_c} = (1 - \sigma(\mathbf{u}_o^T \mathbf{v}_c)) \mathbf{u}_o - \sum_{k=1}^K \sigma(\mathbf{u}_{n_k}^T \mathbf{v}_c) \mathbf{u}_{n_k}}$$

Interpretation: Gradient descent pushes the center vector $\mathbf{v}_c$ towards the true context vector $\mathbf{u}_o$ while repelling it away from all $K$ negative samples simultaneously!

Topic 32: Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA) & SVD Dimensionality Reduction

LSA (Deerwester et al. 1990) factorizes a Term-Document co-occurrence matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times |D|}$ using Singular Value Decomposition:

$$\mathbf{\mathbf{X} \approx \mathbf{U}_k \mathbf{\Sigma}_k \mathbf{V}_k^T}$$
  • $\mathbf{U}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times k}$: Dense $k$-dimensional word representation vectors.
  • $\mathbf{\Sigma}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}$: Diagonal matrix of top $k$ singular values (strengths of latent topics).
  • $\mathbf{V}_k^T \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times |D|}$: Dense $k$-dimensional document representation vectors.
  • Limitation: Computationally cubic $O(|V| \cdot |D|^2)$ time, and fails to capture non-linear contextual semantics.

Q4. Explain Why Word2Vec Uses the Subsampling of Frequent Words and How the Probability Threshold is Formulated. (8 Marks)
In raw natural text, ubiquitous words like `the`, `of`, `and` occur millions of times, providing near-zero semantic discrimination while consuming massive training compute. Mikolov et al. subsample words during training, discarding word $w_i$ with probability: $$\mathbf{P(\text{discard } w_i) = 1 - \sqrt{\frac{t}{f(w_i)}}}$$ Where $f(w_i)$ is the corpus frequency fraction of word $w_i$ and $t \approx 10^{-4}$ is a threshold parameter. Words with frequency $f(w) > t$ are discarded with high probability, accelerating training speed by $2\times$ to $10\times$ while drastically improving vector representations of rare words!

🏛️ Solved Problem 12: Continuous Bag of Words (CBOW) Hidden-to-Output Weight Update

In CBOW, let context words be $\{w_1, \dots, w_{2C}\}$ and true target center word be $w_t$ ($j^*$).

  • Hidden representation: $\mathbf{h} = \frac{1}{2C} \sum_{i=1}^{2C} \mathbf{v}_{w_i} \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
  • Output logits: $z_j = \mathbf{u}_j^T \mathbf{h}$ for all vocabulary words $j \in \{1, \dots, |V|\}$.
  • Predicted probability via softmax: $\hat{y}_j = \frac{\exp(\mathbf{u}_j^T \mathbf{h})}{\sum_k \exp(\mathbf{u}_k^T \mathbf{h})}$.
  • Cross-entropy loss: $\mathcal{L} = -\log \hat{y}_{j^*}$.
  • Output weight gradient: $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{u}_j} = (\hat{y}_j - y_j)\mathbf{h}$ where $y_j = 1$ if $j=j^*$ else $0$.
  • Input weight gradient for each context word $w_i$: $\mathbf{\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{v}_{w_i}} = \frac{1}{2C} \sum_{j=1}^{|V|} (\hat{y}_j - y_j) \mathbf{u}_j}$.
Q5. Explain the Geometric Intuition of Word Vector Analogies and the Cosine Offset Method. (8 Marks)
Word vectors trained on large corpora organize semantic relationships as linear directional vector offsets in $\mathbb{R}^d$: $$\mathbf{v}_{\text{King}} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{Man}} \approx \mathbf{v}_{\text{Queen}} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{Woman}} \implies \mathbf{v}_{\text{King}} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{Man}} + \mathbf{v}_{\text{Woman}} \approx \mathbf{v}_{\text{Queen}}$$ To solve an analogy $a : b :: c : ?$, 3CosAdd finds word $d^*$ maximizing: $$\mathbf{d^* = \arg\max_{x \notin \{a, b, c\}} \cos(\mathbf{v}_x, \mathbf{v}_b - \mathbf{v}_a + \mathbf{v}_c) = \arg\max_x \left[ \cos(\mathbf{v}_x, \mathbf{v}_b) - \cos(\mathbf{v}_x, \mathbf{v}_a) + \cos(\mathbf{v}_x, \mathbf{v}_c) \right]}$$ Levy & Goldberg (2014) introduced 3CosMul to prevent single large similarity terms from dominating: $$\mathbf{d^* = \arg\max_x \frac{\cos(\mathbf{v}_x, \mathbf{v}_b) \cdot \cos(\mathbf{v}_x, \mathbf{v}_c)}{\cos(\mathbf{v}_x, \mathbf{v}_a) + \epsilon}}$$

🏛️ Solved Problem 17: GloVe Log-Bilinear Model Derivation from Co-occurrence Probability Ratios

Let $P_{ik} = P(k \mid i) = \frac{X_{ik}}{X_i}$ be the conditional probability of context word $k$ given word $i$. Consider probe word $k = \text{"solid"}$ with $i = \text{"ice"}$ and $j = \text{"steam"}$:

  • $P(\text{solid} \mid \text{ice}) \approx \text{Large}$, while $P(\text{solid} \mid \text{steam}) \approx \text{Small} \implies \frac{P_{ik}}{P_{jk}} \gg 1.0$.
  • For probe word $k = \text{"gas"}$: $\frac{P(\text{gas} \mid \text{ice})}{P(\text{gas} \mid \text{steam})} \ll 1.0$.
  • For noise word $k = \text{"water"}$: $\frac{P(\text{water} \mid \text{ice})}{P(\text{water} \mid \text{steam})} \approx 1.0$.
  • To encode this ratio linearly in vector space, the relationship must satisfy homomorphism $F(\mathbf{w}_i - \mathbf{w}_j, \mathbf{\tilde{w}}_k) = \frac{P_{ik}}{P_{jk}}$. Setting $F = \exp$: $$\mathbf{\mathbf{w}_i^T \mathbf{\tilde{w}}_k - \mathbf{w}_j^T \mathbf{\tilde{w}}_k = \ln P_{ik} - \ln P_{jk} = \ln X_{ik} - \ln X_i - (\ln X_{jk} - \ln X_j)}$$ Absorbing $\ln X_i$ into scalar bias terms $b_i, \tilde{b}_k$ yields the canonical GloVe objective: $$\mathbf{\mathbf{w}_i^T \mathbf{\tilde{w}}_k + b_i + \tilde{b}_k = \ln X_{ik}}$$
Q7. Detail the FastText Hashing Trick for Storing Millions of Subword N-Grams in Memory. (8 Marks)
In morphologically rich languages, the number of unique character n-grams (sizes 3 to 6) easily exceeds 10 million distinct strings, requiring gigabytes of RAM for embedding lookup tables.
• The Hashing Trick (Fowler-Noll-Vo / MurmurHash): Instead of storing a dictionary mapping strings to integers, FastText hashes each character n-gram string directly into a fixed-size integer bucket array of size $K = 2 \times 10^6$ using hash function $h(g) = \text{hash}(g) \pmod K$. Multiple rare subwords map to shared embedding buckets, providing massive parameter compression with negligible accuracy degradation!

🏛️ Solved Problem 28: Word2Vec Continuous Bag of Words (CBOW) Computational Complexity

For training corpus of $T$ words, vocabulary size $|V|$, embedding dimension $d$, and context window size $C$:

  • Input-to-Hidden projection: $2C \times d$ lookups $\implies O(C \cdot d)$.
  • Hidden-to-Output with Standard Softmax: $|V| \times d$ operations $\implies \mathbf{O(T \cdot (C \cdot d + |V| \cdot d))}$.
  • Hidden-to-Output with Negative Sampling ($K$ samples): $(K + 1) \times d$ operations $\implies \mathbf{O(T \cdot (C \cdot d + K \cdot d))}$.
  • $$\mathbf{\text{Speedup with Negative Sampling: } \frac{|V|}{K} \approx \frac{100,000}{5} = \mathbf{20,000\times \text{ Faster!}}}$$
