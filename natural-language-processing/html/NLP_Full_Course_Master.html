Topic 3 & 4: Subword Tokenization (Byte-Pair Encoding - BPE)

The Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) Algorithm (Sennrich et al., 2016)
  1. Initialize vocabulary with all individual characters in the training corpus plus an end-of-word symbol ``.
  2. Count the frequency of all adjacent symbol pairs in the tokenized corpus.
  3. Identify the most frequent symbol pair $(c_1, c_2)$ and merge it into a single new vocabulary token $c_1 c_2$.
  4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 for a fixed number of merge operations $k$ (e.g., $32,000$ merges in GPT-2).
  5. Significance: Solves the Out-of-Vocabulary (OOV) problem by representing rare and unseen words as sequences of subword units (e.g., `unaffordability` $\rightarrow$ `un` + `afford` + `ability`).

Topic 6 & 7: $N$-gram Language Modeling & The Chain Rule

1. Exact Chain Rule of Probability for Sentence $W = (w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n)$: $$P(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n) = \prod_{k=1}^n P(w_k \mid w_1, w_2, \dots, w_{k-1})$$
2. Bigram Language Model (1st-Order Markov Assumption): $$P(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n) \approx \prod_{k=1}^n P(w_k \mid w_{k-1}) \quad \text{where } P(w_k \mid w_{k-1}) = \frac{C(w_{k-1}, w_k)}{C(w_{k-1})}$$
3. Language Model Perplexity ($\text{PPL}$): $$\text{PPL}(W) = P(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n)^{-\frac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{\frac{1}{P(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n)}} = 2^{-\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \log_2 P(w_i \mid w_{i-1})}$$ Lower Perplexity indicates a superior language model that is less surprised by the test corpus!

Topic 9 – 11: Smoothing Techniques for Statistical Language Models

Smoothing Method Probability Estimation Formula Key Mechanics & Intuition
1. Laplace (Add-1) $$P_{\text{Laplace}}(w_i \mid w_{i-1}) = \frac{C(w_{i-1}, w_i) + 1}{C(w_{i-1}) + V}$$ Adds 1 to every count; shifts far too much probability mass to unseen $N$-grams in large vocabularies $V$.
2. Add-$k$ (Lidstone) $$P_{\text{Add-}k}(w_i \mid w_{i-1}) = \frac{C(w_{i-1}, w_i) + k}{C(w_{i-1}) + k V} \quad (0 < k < 1)$$ Reduces probability mass distortion compared to Add-1.
3. Absolute Discounting $$P(w_i \mid w_{i-1}) = \frac{\max(C(w_{i-1}, w_i) - d, 0)}{C(w_{i-1})} + \frac{d \cdot |\{w : C(w_{i-1}, w) > 0\}|}{C(w_{i-1})} P(w_i)$$ Subtracts a fixed discount $d \in (0, 1)$ from non-zero counts and redistributes mass to unigrams.
4. Kneser-Ney (Interpolated) $$P_{\text{KN}}(w_i \mid w_{i-1}) = \frac{\max(C(w_{i-1}, w_i) - d, 0)}{C(w_{i-1})} + \lambda(w_{i-1}) P_{\text{continuation}}(w_i)$$ $$P_{\text{continuation}}(w_i) = \frac{|\{w' : C(w', w_i) > 0\}|}{\sum_{w} |\{w' : C(w', w) > 0\}|}$$ Considers how versatile word $w_i$ is as a continuation across diverse preceding contexts (e.g., `San Francisco`). State-of-the-art $N$-gram smoothing.

Q1. Define Perplexity. Given a test sentence with probability $10^{-6}$ and length 6 words, compute its Perplexity. (6 Marks)
Perplexity ($\text{PPL}$): The reciprocal geometric mean of the probability assigned to the test sentence by the language model:
$$\text{PPL}(W) = (P(W))^{-1/N} = (10^{-6})^{-1/6} = 10^1 = \mathbf{10}$$ A perplexity of 10 means the model is as confused as if it had to choose uniformly among 10 words at each step.

Topic 1 – 4: Part-of-Speech Tagging & HMM Mathematical Architecture

Part-of-Speech (POS) Tagging is the task of assigning a grammatical category label (e.g., Noun, Verb, Adjective, Preposition) to each word in an input text sequence:

$$\mathbf{w} = (w_1, w_2, \dots, w_T) \xrightarrow{\text{POS Tagging}} \mathbf{t} = (t_1, t_2, \dots, t_T)$$
Hidden Markov Model 5-Tuple $\lambda = (S, V, A, B, \pi)$:
  1. $S = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_N\}$: Finite set of hidden states (e.g., POS tags).
  2. $V = \{v_1, v_2, \dots, v_M\}$: Finite set of observable vocabulary symbols (words).
  3. $A = \{a_{ij}\}$: State Transition Probability Matrix, where $a_{ij} = P(q_t = s_j \mid q_{t-1} = s_i)$.
  4. $B = \{b_j(k)\}$: Emission Probability Matrix, where $b_j(k) = P(o_t = v_k \mid q_t = s_j)$.
  5. $\pi = \{\pi_i\}$: Initial State Probability Distribution, where $\pi_i = P(q_1 = s_i)$.

Topic 7 & 8: The Viterbi Dynamic Programming Decoding Algorithm

Viterbi Recurrence Relations: Let $v_t(j)$ be the highest probability of any state sequence ending in state $j$ at time $t$: 1. Initialization ($t = 1$): $$v_1(j) = \pi_j \cdot b_j(o_1), \quad \text{backpointer}_1(j) = 0 \quad (1 \le j \le N)$$ 2. Recursion ($t = 2 \dots T$): $$v_t(j) = \max_{i=1}^N \Big( v_{t-1}(i) \cdot a_{ij} \Big) \cdot b_j(o_t) \quad (1 \le j \le N)$$ $$\text{backpointer}_t(j) = \arg\max_{i=1}^N \Big( v_{t-1}(i) \cdot a_{ij} \Big)$$ 3. Termination: $$P^* = \max_{i=1}^N v_T(i), \quad q_T^* = \arg\max_{i=1}^N v_T(i)$$ 4. Backtracking: $$q_t^* = \text{backpointer}_{t+1}(q_{t+1}^*) \quad \text{for } t = T-1, T-2, \dots, 1$$

Topic 10 & 11: MEMMs vs. Linear-Chain CRFs (Label Bias Problem)

Model Class Mathematical Formulation Normalization & Key Theoretical Properties
1. Generative HMM $$P(\mathbf{w}, \mathbf{t}) = \prod_{i=1}^T P(t_i \mid t_{i-1}) P(w_i \mid t_i)$$ Models joint probability; cannot easily integrate overlapping, non-independent features.
2. Discriminative MEMM $$P(\mathbf{t} \mid \mathbf{w}) = \prod_{i=1}^T \frac{\exp(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{f}(t_i, t_{i-1}, \mathbf{w}, i))}{\sum_{t'} \exp(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{f}(t', t_{i-1}, \mathbf{w}, i))}$$ Local Normalization at each step: Suffers from the Label Bias Problem where states with low-entropy transitions dominate regardless of input observations.
3. Linear-Chain CRF $$P(\mathbf{t} \mid \mathbf{w}) = \frac{1}{Z(\mathbf{w})} \exp\left( \sum_{i=1}^T \sum_k w_k f_k(t_i, t_{i-1}, \mathbf{w}, i) \right)$$ Global Normalization ($Z(\mathbf{w})$): Overcomes the Label Bias Problem completely by evaluating the total score of the entire sequence simultaneously.

Q1. State the 3 fundamental problems of Hidden Markov Models and name the algorithms used to solve them. (6 Marks)
1. Evaluation Problem (Likelihood): Given model $\lambda$ and observation sequence $O$, compute $P(O \mid \lambda)$. Solved by the Forward Algorithm (or Backward Algorithm) in $O(N^2 T)$ time.
2. Decoding Problem: Given model $\lambda$ and observation sequence $O$, find the optimal hidden state sequence $Q^*$ that best explains $O$. Solved by the Viterbi Algorithm in $O(N^2 T)$ time.
3. Learning Problem (Parameter Estimation): Given observation sequence $O$, adjust model parameters $\lambda = (A, B, \pi)$ to maximize $P(O \mid \lambda)$. Solved by the Baum-Welch (EM) Algorithm.

Topic 4 & 5: Chomsky Normal Form (CNF) & The CYK Algorithm

A Context-Free Grammar is in Chomsky Normal Form (CNF) if every production rule has one of two forms:

$$A \rightarrow B \ C \quad \text{or} \quad A \rightarrow a$$

Where $A, B, C$ are non-terminals ($B, C \neq S$) and $a$ is a single terminal symbol.

CYK Dynamic Programming Recurrence: Let table entry $P[i, j, A]$ be true if non-terminal $A$ derives substring $w_i \dots w_j$ of length $l = j - i + 1$: 1. Base Case (Length $l = 1$): $$P[i, i, A] = \text{true} \quad \text{for all } A \rightarrow w_i \in P$$ 2. Inductive Step (Length $l = 2 \dots n$): $$P[i, j, A] = \text{true} \iff \exists k \in [i, j-1] \text{ and } A \rightarrow B \ C \in P \text{ such that } P[i, k, B] = \text{true} \land P[k+1, j, C] = \text{true}$$ Time Complexity: $O(n^3 \cdot |P|)$ where $n$ is sentence length and $|P|$ is number of CNF grammar rules.

Topic 9 & 10: Dependency Parsing & Transition Systems

Dependency Syntax describes grammatical structure by establishing direct binary asymmetric grammatical relations between words (Heads $\rightarrow$ Dependents), without intermediate phrase nodes:

Transition Action Stack & Buffer Manipulation Dependency Arc Created
1. SHIFT Remove first word $w$ from Buffer and push onto Stack. None.
2. LEFT-ARC ($r$) Creates dependency arc $s_1 \xleftarrow{r} s_2$ from top of stack $s_1$ to second item $s_2$. Pops $s_2$ from stack. $s_1 \rightarrow s_2$ ($s_1$ is Head).
3. RIGHT-ARC ($r$) Creates dependency arc $s_1 \xrightarrow{r} s_2$ from second item $s_1$ to top of stack $s_2$. Pops $s_2$ from stack. $s_1 \rightarrow s_2$ ($s_1$ is Head).

🏛️ Top BIT Mesra Exam Questions & Answers (Module III)

Q1. State the Chomsky Normal Form (CNF) restrictions and explain why CNF is mandatory for the CYK algorithm. (8 Marks)
- CNF Restrictions: All productions must strictly be of the form $A \rightarrow BC$ (exactly 2 non-terminals) or $A \rightarrow a$ (exactly 1 terminal). No unit productions ($A \rightarrow B$) or $\epsilon$-productions ($A \rightarrow \epsilon$) are allowed.
- Why Mandatory for CYK: CYK relies on dynamic programming by splitting a substring into exactly two parts ($w[i \dots k]$ and $w[k+1 \dots j]$). Binary branching ($A \rightarrow BC$) guarantees that any subphrase is formed by joining exactly two adjacent subphrases, creating the $O(n^3)$ tabular recurrence.

Topic 4 & 5: Vector Space Models (TF-IDF & Positive PMI)

1. Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency ($\text{TF-IDF}$): $$\text{TF-IDF}(t, d, D) = \text{TF}(t, d) \times \text{IDF}(t, D)$$ $$\text{TF}(t, d) = \frac{f_{t, d}}{\sum_{t' \in d} f_{t', d}}, \quad \text{IDF}(t, D) = \log_{10}\left( \frac{|D|}{|\{d \in D : t \in d\}|} \right)$$
2. Positive Pointwise Mutual Information ($\text{PPMI}$): $$\text{PMI}(w, c) = \log_2\left( \frac{P(w, c)}{P(w) P(c)} \right) = \log_2\left( \frac{C(w, c) \cdot N}{C(w) \cdot C(c)} \right)$$ $$\text{PPMI}(w, c) = \max(\text{PMI}(w, c), 0)$$

Topic 7 & 8: Word2Vec (Skip-Gram with Negative Sampling - SGNS)

Word2Vec learns dense low-dimensional continuous vector embeddings ($d \approx 300$) where semantically similar words are mapped to proximate points in vector space.

Skip-Gram with Negative Sampling (SGNS) Loss Function
For a target word $w$ with true context word $c_{\text{pos}}$ and $k$ randomly drawn negative words $c_{\text{neg}, 1 \dots k}$ from noise distribution $P_n(w) \propto f(w)^{3/4}$: $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SGNS}} = \log \sigma(\mathbf{v}_{c_{\text{pos}}}^T \mathbf{v}_w) + \sum_{i=1}^k \mathbb{E}_{c_{\text{neg}, i} \sim P_n} \Big[ \log \sigma(-\mathbf{v}_{c_{\text{neg}, i}}^T \mathbf{v}_w) \Big]$$ Where $\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$ is the logistic sigmoid function. Maximizes dot product with actual context words while pushing away negative noise samples!

Topic 10: GloVe (Global Vectors for Word Representation)

GloVe Log-Bilinear Objective Function (Pennington, Socher, Manning, 2014): $$J = \sum_{i, j=1}^V f(X_{ij}) \Big( \mathbf{w}_i^T \tilde{\mathbf{w}}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j - \log X_{ij} \Big)^2$$ Where $X_{ij}$ is the co-occurrence count of word $i$ with context word $j$, and $f(X_{ij}) = \min(1, (X_{ij} / x_{\max})^\alpha)$ is a weighting function that caps the influence of extremely frequent stop words (with $\alpha = 0.75, x_{\max} = 100$).

Q1. Compare CBOW and Skip-Gram models in Word2Vec across 4 architectural parameters. (8 Marks)
1. Prediction Task: CBOW predicts the target center word given a window of surrounding context words; Skip-Gram predicts the surrounding context words given the center word.
2. Training Speed: CBOW trains several times faster than Skip-Gram because context word vectors are averaged in a single forward pass.
3. Performance on Rare Words: Skip-Gram is substantially superior for rare words and small datasets because each target-context pair is treated as a separate training sample.
4. Vector Representations: CBOW smooths over context distribution; Skip-Gram preserves multi-sense representations.

Topic 2 – 4: The Transformer & Scaled Dot-Product Attention

1. Scaled Dot-Product Attention: $$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left( \frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} \right) V$$ Where $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_k}$ is the Query matrix, $K \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_k}$ is the Key matrix, and $V \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_v}$ is the Value matrix.
Why Scale by $\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}$? For large values of $d_k$, dot products grow large in magnitude, pushing the softmax function into regions with extremely small gradients. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ stabilizes gradient backpropagation!
2. Multi-Head Attention: $$\text{MultiHead}(Q, K, V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \text{head}_2, \dots, \text{head}_h) W^O$$ $$\text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(Q W_i^Q, K W_i^K, V W_i^V)$$ Where projection parameter matrices $W_i^Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k}, W_i^K \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k}, W_i^V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_v}$, and $W^O \in \mathbb{R}^{h d_v \times d_{\text{model}}}$.

1. 🏛️ Top BIT Mesra Exam Questions & Answers (Module V)

Q1. Explain why Self-Attention overcomes the fundamental sequential bottleneck of RNNs and LSTMs. (8 Marks)
1. Massive Parallelization: RNNs compute hidden state $h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t)$ sequentially, requiring $O(n)$ sequential time steps that cannot be parallelized across GPU cores. Self-Attention computes all pairwise token interactions simultaneously via matrix multiplications ($QK^T$) in $O(1)$ sequential operations.
2. Direct Long-Range Paths: In RNNs, information must travel step-by-step through $n$ recurrent transitions, causing vanishing gradients for distant words. Self-Attention connects any two positions in the sequence with a direct $O(1)$ path length, enabling instantaneous long-range context resolution.
